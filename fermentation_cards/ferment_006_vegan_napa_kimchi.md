# Vegan Napa Cabbage Kimchi

## Ingredients

Percentages are calculated against the 2000 g of prepared napa cabbage. The salted shrimp and anchovy fish sauce of the traditional card are replaced here by a shiitake and kombu umami base.

| Ingredient | Weight | Percentage of cabbage |
|---|---|---|
| Napa cabbage | 2000 g | 100% |
| Coarse sea salt (for brining) | 160 g | 8% |
| Gochugaru (Korean chilli flakes) | 100 g | 5% |
| Korean radish, julienned | 400 g | 20% |
| Garlic, minced | 60 g | 3% |
| Ginger, grated | 20 g | 1% |
| Dried shiitake mushrooms | 30 g | 1.5% |
| Kombu (dried kelp) | 10 g | 0.5% |
| Tamari (gluten-free soy sauce) | 50 g | 2.5% |
| Glutinous rice flour | 30 g | 1.5% |
| Caster sugar | 20 g | 1% |

Total yield is approximately 2.85 kg of finished kimchi.

## Method

Brine the quartered cabbage with the full 160 g of coarse sea salt for 4 to 6 hours, turning every 2 hours, then apply the bend test: a thick white stem should bend into a U without snapping. Rinse three times and drain cut side down for one hour.

Build the umami base while the cabbage brines. Simmer the dried shiitake and the kombu in 400 ml of water for 20 minutes, then remove the kombu before it turns slimy and simmer the shiitake for a further 10 minutes. Strain, reserving 250 ml of the liquid, and finely chop the rehydrated shiitake caps to fold into the paste.

This shiitake and kombu stock plus the tamari is what replaces the saeujeot and anchovy fish sauce used on the traditional card. Both supply glutamates, which is the savoury depth the fermented seafood would otherwise provide.

Whisk the glutinous rice flour into the reserved 250 ml of mushroom stock, heat gently for 5 minutes until thickened, stir in the sugar and cool completely.

Mix the cooled porridge with the gochugaru and rest for 10 minutes, then add the garlic, ginger, chopped shiitake and tamari. Fold through the julienned radish.

Coat every leaf with the paste, pack into a jar with 5 cm of headspace, and press down until the brine rises above the cabbage.

Ferment at 20C for 2 to 3 days, which is around a day longer than the traditional card requires because the seafood ferments in saeujeot give the traditional version a head start. Move to the refrigerator once bubbling and pleasantly sour.

## Allergen Note

Contains no fish, no shellfish, no dairy, no egg, no nuts and no gluten. Tamari is used specifically in place of ordinary soy sauce because ordinary soy sauce is brewed with wheat. Suitable for vegans and vegetarians.
